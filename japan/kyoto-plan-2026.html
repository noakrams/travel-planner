The planning brief

Iconic, green, and paced like a holiday.

This keeps the places that make a first Kyoto visit feel complete, but refuses the common “six temples before lunch” mistake. Each full day has two real anchors, a proper break, and a third element that can be dropped without damaging the day.

Day by day

The plan

Times are deliberately realistic rather than minute-perfect. “Must” protects the day’s identity, “recommended” is worth the effort, and “optional” is the first thing to release when heat, queues, or tired feet intervene.

24Thursday

Tokyo → Nishiki → teamLab

A travel day that still feels like Kyoto, without asking you to wake before dawn.

6–8 km
mostly level
09:30
Recommended

Depart Tokyo on a direct Nozomi

Target a train around 09:30–10:00. Typical ride time is a little over two hours; reserve seats rather than improvising immediately after Silver Week.

12:15

Taxi to Hotel Resol and leave luggage

The hotel is in the middle of the evening action, not by Kyoto Station. Budget roughly 20 minutes door to door before check-in time.

12:45
Food break

Nishiki Market grazing lunch

Walk through “Kyoto’s kitchen” while stalls are open: grilled bites, rice crackers, fruit, sweets, and something substantial before teamLab. Ask about dashi; “vegetable” does not automatically mean fish-free.

14:15

Taxi or train to the Kyoto Station area. Arrive early enough to use the lockers and enter calmly.

14:30–18:00
MustTimed ticket

teamLab Biovortex Kyoto

Protect 3–3½ hours. The museum is much larger than the 90-minute teamLab assumption and recent reports repeatedly call it Kyoto’s strongest indoor experience. Wear sneakers; some Athletic Forest works reject sandals or unstable footwear. Official tickets begin at ¥3,800 and can be changed up to three times under the official conditions.

18:30
Rest

Check in and reset

Shower, unpack, and take 45–60 minutes off your feet.

20:00
Recommended

Easy first dinner near the hotel

Katsukura Sanjo is the dependable first-night choice: a calm Kyoto-born tonkatsu restaurant a few minutes away, open to 21:00 and requiring no reservation. Take a short lantern-lit look at Kiyamachi or Pontocho afterward only if energy remains.

Why this order

teamLab is seven minutes from Kyoto Station, but luggage over 50 cm cannot enter. Dropping bags and eating first turns the museum into the day’s single major commitment.

Drop if delayed

Skip Nishiki and eat near the museum. Do not compress Biovortex below 2½ hours just to preserve the market.

25Friday

Arashiyama beyond the bamboo photo

Nature, a Zen garden, mountain views, and the river—then the Golden Pavilion if the legs agree.

10–13 km
steps & slopes
08:45

Leave after hotel breakfast

Walk to Kyoto-Kawaramachi Station and take the Hankyu line via Katsura. Expected door-to-door time: about 40–50 minutes.

09:40
Must

Bamboo Grove → Ōkōchi Sansō

The grove itself is brief and will already be busy. Walk through it without making it carry the whole day, then enter the former actor’s hillside garden. The ¥1,000 admission includes tea and a sweet; this is the quiet, scenic payoff many crowd-disappointed visitors miss.

11:15
Recommended

Tenryū-ji garden

Enter from the north side and move toward the main gate. Its pond-and-mountain composition makes this garden distinct from the later temple stops. Official hours are 08:30–17:00; garden admission is ¥500.

12:30
Long lunch

Lunch and a proper sit-down

Choose by queue on the day. The useful rule here is more important than a viral restaurant: avoid losing 60–90 minutes to one famous soba shop. Confirm dipping sauce or broth is not fish-based for Noa.

14:00
Recommended

Togetsukyō Bridge and Hozu River edge

A relaxed riverside walk supplies the “scenic Kyoto” that the bamboo lane alone cannot. Sit with a coffee or cold drink before deciding on the north-Kyoto add-on.

15:00
Recommended

Taxi to Kinkaku-ji

Allow roughly 20–30 minutes depending on traffic. The Golden Pavilion is a short, crowded one-way circuit, but you explicitly want the icons and it is visually unique. Official hours are 09:00–17:00; admission ¥500.

16:30
First drop

Return to the hotel

If Arashiyama ran long or the heat is heavy, skip Kinkaku-ji and go straight back. This is the plan’s cleanest sacrifice because it costs substantial transit for a short visit.

19:00

Casual downtown dinner

Keep it unbooked. Kyoto Engine Ramen is the safe mixed-menu option when Noa wants a clearly fish-free vegan bowl and Eshed wants a regular ramen; otherwise browse Teramachi around the hotel.

Why this order

Friday is the least-bad crowd day for Arashiyama. The route passes through the famous lane into quieter terrain, then exits through Tenryū-ji without a separate cross-town temple run.

Walking reality

Ōkōchi Sansō has slopes and stone steps. Take it slowly; if knees object, replace it with a longer river café break and keep Tenryū-ji.

26Saturday

Higashiyama, tea, and the splurge

The classic old-Kyoto day, paced from temple terrace to teahouse to a Taishō-era garden dinner.

9–12 km
hills early
08:30

Taxi to Kiyomizu-dera

This is the one morning worth beginning promptly, but not at 06:00. A taxi avoids the uphill bus-stop walk and delivers you to the top of the district.

09:00
Must

Kiyomizu-dera

See the wooden stage, green Higashiyama backdrop, and Otowa waterfall. It will be busy; the plan accepts that rather than pretending a later start can beat the crowds. Allow 75–90 minutes.

10:30
Must

Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka downhill

Descending is kinder than climbing. Pause for the Yasaka Pagoda view, ceramics, and small food shops, but keep moving toward the booked experience.

12:00
ReserveRecommended

Camellia Flower shared tea ceremony

A 45-minute English experience in a machiya directly on Ninenzaka: watch the host, have a seasonal sweet, and whisk your own matcha. The shared ceremony is the right value; no kimono add-on is needed. Book the Flower shared ceremony on Camellia’s official site ↗

13:00
Lunch

Unhurried lunch in Higashiyama

Take at least an hour. The tea sweet is not lunch, and the special dinner is still five hours away.

14:00
Recommended

Ishibe-kōji → Yasaka Shrine → Gion

A geographically continuous old-Kyoto walk rather than another headline temple. Continue through Maruyama Park and Hanamikoji, treating private lanes and working geiko/maiko respectfully.

15:30
If there’s time

Kennin-ji

Add one quiet Zen interior and garden only if you still want another temple. Otherwise go directly to the hotel for a longer rest.

16:15
Rest

Hotel reset

Allow at least 75 minutes before dinner. Taxi back to Higashiyama around 17:35.

18:00
Special dinnerReserve

LE UN Akagane Resort Kyoto Higashiyama 1925

Kyoto-inspired French cuisine inside a restored Taishō-era villa and garden. The current ¥16,000 tasting course sits inside your splurge range. The restaurant explicitly offers vegetarian/vegan and allergen-aware menus with at least three days’ notice: request a fish-, seafood-, and fish-dashi-free menu for Noa, while Eshed keeps the standard seasonal menu.

21:00
Optional bar

Bar K6

If this is the bar night, stop for one well-made drink near the hotel. K6 is the quieter classic choice, open 18:00–02:00 and closed Monday–Tuesday. Otherwise walk the short stretch of Pontocho and call it a night.

Why this order

All daytime sights form one downhill line. The only deliberate repeat journey is the hotel rest before dinner—and the taxi is worth it.

Rain handling

Keep the tea ceremony and dinner. Shorten the lanes, use taxis at both ends, and move Kennin-ji from optional to recommended for its covered interiors.

27Sunday

Fushimi Inari + the Nara worth doing

Nara is not a deer-only full day: the Great Buddha gives it weight, and the rail corridor makes Fushimi a rational pairing.

11–14 km
some stairs
08:45

Keihan train south

Walk or take a short taxi to Gion-Shijo, then ride directly to Fushimi-Inari Station. This is easier from your hotel than backtracking to Kyoto Station.

09:20
Must

Fushimi Inari Taisha

See the main shrine and Senbon Torii. At this hour the lower route will be crowded, but people thin as the path climbs. Do not make the summit mandatory: the lower loop gives you the iconic experience in about 75 minutes.

10:40
Energy option

Continue to Yotsutsuji

Add roughly 45–60 minutes and meaningful stairs for the halfway view. Only do this if both of you feel fresh; Nara still involves a long park walk.

11:10

JR Inari → Nara

Cross to JR Inari Station and continue on the Nara line. Allow about an hour depending on the local/rapid connection.

12:15
Lunch

Lunch before the deer

Eat near Kintetsu Nara or the west edge of the park before buying deer crackers. A café with pasta, curry, or a meat dish gives Noa a clearer fish-free lunch than Nara’s famous persimmon-leaf sushi or noodle broth.

13:15
Recommended

Nara Park and deer

Enjoy the novelty, but keep it to roughly 45 minutes. The deer are wild: feed only official crackers, show empty hands when finished, and do not tease them with food.

14:00
Must

Tōdai-ji Great Buddha Hall

This is why the Nara trip survives the “overrated” concern. The scale is categorically different from Kyoto’s intimate temples. Official September hours are 07:30–17:30; admission is ¥800.

15:15
If there’s time

Nigatsu-dō viewpoint

A quieter terrace east of the Great Buddha Hall. It adds stairs but gives a final landscape view. Skip it if you climbed to Yotsutsuji in the morning.

16:15

Return to Kyoto

Use a park bus if feet are tired, then Kintetsu to Kyoto or JR depending on the best live connection. Expect to reach the hotel around 17:30–18:00.

19:30

Flexible final Kyoto dinner

No reservation. Choose by appetite near Kawaramachi: ramen, gyoza, yakitori, or a department-store food hall picnic. Pack afterward for the Osaka move.

Why not add Uji?

Fushimi + Nara already reaches your upper walking range. Uji would turn the day into a rail checklist and duplicate tea after the dedicated ceremony.

Known closure

Isuien would normally fit you perfectly, but its official site closes the garden for maintenance in the last third of September. It is intentionally absent.

28Monday

Kyoto → Osaka

Leave while the trip still feels unhurried.

transfer day
08:00
Easy morning

Breakfast and checkout

Breakfast is included. Check out by 09:30–10:00, comfortably ahead of the hotel’s 11:00 deadline.

10:00

Train to Osaka

The exact route depends on the Osaka hotel: Hankyu is convenient for Umeda; Keihan plus metro can suit some central areas; JR is simplest if the hotel is near Osaka Station. Finalize after the Osaka booking is confirmed.

Why nothing else

You asked for a fairly early—but not painfully early—Osaka move. This avoids sightseeing with luggage and gives Osaka a genuine arrival day.

Luggage

No forwarding is needed for this short leg unless the Osaka hotel cannot hold bags. Carrying cases on an off-peak late-morning train is reasonable.

Food without making Noa compromise

A small, useful bench

The plan avoids over-reserving ordinary meals. These options cover different moods near places you will already be, and all give Noa a clear path away from fish while leaving Eshed unrestricted.

Katsukura Sanjo

Kyoto-born tonkatsu in a calm hidden dining room, very close to the hotel. No reservations; official hours 11:00–21:00. Best first-night fallback.

Casual · roughly ¥1,500–3,000 pp

Kyoto Engine Ramen

Regular ramen alongside clearly labeled vegan miso and edamame-based bowls. Popular with international visitors because mixed-diet couples can both eat properly.

Casual · around ¥1,800–2,200 pp

Nishiki Market

Use it as a shared tasting lunch, not a rigid restaurant stop. Eshed can try seafood; Noa can build a meal from grilled meat, rice snacks, fruit, sweets, and vendors able to confirm ingredients.

Grazing · set your own budget

Bar K6

A grown-up cocktail bar near the hotel rather than a late club. Official hours 18:00–02:00, closed Monday and Tuesday. Slot it only if you want the optional bar night.

Drinks · check menu pricing on the night

The recurring trap is dashi. Bonito stock appears in noodle broth, dipping sauce, miso soup, rolled omelet, and apparently vegetable dishes. The useful request is “no fish, seafood, or fish-based dashi,” not simply “no visible fish.”

Weather flexibility

One switch, not five backup plans

Late September remains hot, humid, and inside typhoon season. The plan is structured, but it only needs one clean weather swap.

If Friday is the clearest day

Keep the plan as written. Arashiyama benefits most from dry paths and open mountain views.

If Friday is the wettest day

Swap Friday and Sunday: do Fushimi’s lower route plus Tōdai-ji in rain, and save Arashiyama’s garden, slopes, and river for Sunday. Keep Saturday fixed because tea and dinner are booked.

What the research changed

Verdicts, not popularity scores

Operational facts come from official sources. Community reports are used for pacing, disappointment patterns, and practical friction—not treated as proof.

Strong cross-source convergence

Arashiyama is worthwhile; the bamboo lane alone is not.

Traveler reports repeatedly describe the grove as brief and crowded. Tenryū-ji, Ōkōchi Sansō, and the river make the west-Kyoto trip earn half a day.

Mixed opinion · retained for your preference

Kinkaku-ji is a beautiful image and a thin experience.

It remains because you want icons. It is also the first drop because the visit is short and its location costs time.

Recent reports · mostly positive

Biovortex deserves three hours.

Adult couples and first-time teamLab visitors commonly call it a highlight or their favorite teamLab. Dissent centers on crowds, phones, dizziness, and the large time cost.

Official structure + broad community pattern

Nara is a half day, but not merely a deer stop.

The deer novelty can wear off; Tōdai-ji’s Great Buddha is the high-value anchor. Fushimi sits on the same southern rail corridor, making the combination efficient.

Facebook check · useful price distinction

Camellia Flower shared is the sensible version—not Garden private.

In the Hebrew groups, travelers pushed back on the high price of Camellia Garden’s private ceremony, while two commenters specifically identified the Flower/Gion group ceremony as the much cheaper option; one reported paying about ₪100 per person. That matches Camellia’s current ¥4,000 September price and supports the version used here.

Mr Japan · useful selectively

Use taxis strategically and expect September heat.

Mr Japan’s destination material is thin, but its practical guidance explicitly treats short Kyoto taxi rides as legitimate and lists September around 29/21°C with meaningful rain/typhoon risk. This aligns with official/logistical evidence.
